Henmi Sōsuke ([逸見宗助](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%80%B8%E8%A6%8B%E5%AE%97%E5%8A%A9), 1843–1894)[^year] was one of the first three swordsmen hired into the Keishichō gekken sewakari (撃剣世話掛) in 1879 and the man through whom [Tatsumi-ryū](../ryuha/tatsumi-ryu.md) (立身流) entered Meiji police swordsmanship. [Yamaoka Tesshū](yamaoka-tesshu.md) is said to have judged that of the many swordsmen of the day, "only Henmi wields the true sword."[^birth]

Born a samurai of the Sakura domain, he was the son of Henmi Nobutaka, seventeenth-generation head of Tatsumi-ryū, and trained in the family art — an old style fought with a leather-covered shinai. When the domain, following the spread of shinai-and-armor gekken in Edo, permitted other-school study in 1850 and invited [Hokushin Ittō-ryū](../ryuha/hokushin-ryu.md) and [Kyōshin Meichi-ryū](../ryuha/kyoshin-meichi-ryu.md) teachers, Henmi received Kyōshin Meichi-ryū instruction under [Ueda Umanosuke](ueda-umanosuke.md). In 1861 he went to Edo — first to the Hokushin Ittō-ryū of Chiba Eijirō, then to Momonoi Shunzō's Shigakukan, where the grounding of his Tatsumi-ryū training carried him to the position of juku-head in under a year — before returning to serve his domain ([jawiki_henmi](../references.md#jawiki_henmi)).

After the Restoration he was working the Yachimata reclamation land when, in 1879, the Keishichō established the gekken sewakari; he was among the first three recruited, with Ueda Umanosuke and [Kajikawa Yoshimasa](kajikawa-yoshimasa.md). As one of the central figures of the metropolitan force he took part in compiling the Keishi-ryū swordsmanship, iai, and jūjutsu, and Tatsumi-ryū technique entered the composite — the wrist-lock throw *makiotoshi* (巻落) among the sword forms and *shihō* (四方; "four directions") among the iai ([tatsumiryu_keishichoryu](../references.md#tatsumiryu_keishichoryu); [jawiki_keishiryu](../references.md#jawiki_keishiryu)).[^tatsumi]



In December 1884, when the Shiga governor Koteda Yasusada brought a body of Kansai swordsmen to challenge the Keishichō and thirty-odd sewakari were beaten in succession by [Takayama Minesaburō](takayama-minesaburo.md), it was Henmi who took the final stand, winning from his favored upper stance with a wrist strike and saving the force's honor. The Kurume master [Matsuzaki Namishirō](matsuzaki-namishiro.md) is separately recorded as having defeated him in an imperial-viewing match. He died in 1894.

## End Notes

[^year]:  His birth year is given as 1843 (Tenpō 14) or, in some accounts, 1834 (Tenpō 5).

[^birth]: The entry draws on the Japanese Wikipedia article for Henmi and the Tatsumi-ryū Sōhonbu site; the latter is partial to the Tatsumi-ryū role but usefully cites the dated 1888 grade-list and the Keishi-ryū form-by-form adoptions. Yamaoka's "only Henmi wields the true sword" verdict is a traditional judgment repeated across the popular accounts. ([jawiki_henmi](../references.md#jawiki_henmi); [tatsumiryu_keishichoryu](../references.md#tatsumiryu_keishichoryu))

[^tatsumi]: The exact relation of his two lines — Tatsumi-ryū by inheritance and Kyōshin Meichi-ryū by training — in what he actually taught at the Keishichō remains unclear.
