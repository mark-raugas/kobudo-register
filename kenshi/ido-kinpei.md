## Summary

Ido Kinpei (井戸金平) was a [Hōzōin-ryū](../ryuha/hozoin-ryu.md) spearman attached to the Kōbusho, a pupil of the instructor
[Komai Hangorō](komai-hangoro.md), and about thirty years old in the years when the academy
made bouting rather than *kata* the substance of its practice. He is worth a record for a reason that
has nothing to do with rank: the fullest surviving account of what actually happened on the Kōbusho
floor is an account of his matches. He appears on the roster in the *sewa-kokoroe* tier of the spear
department, entered as of the *kobushingumi* under Ogasawara Yahachirō and as the third son of
Shin'emon.[^roster]

## What he did

His method was a deliberate refusal of the decorous exchange. With only about two *shaku* of the
spear's point showing, he would press inside his opponent's hands, and on the beat of the thrust take
him down with *ashigarami* (足搦; a foot-entanglement).[^tsg39]

Every instructor lost to it, and the complaints never stopped. Each time, Kinpei answered by speaking
of what decides victory and defeat in a real fight, and would not entertain the objection at all —
which made him, in the chronicler's phrase, very hard going for the teaching staff. The narrator adds,
fairly, that leaving the *ashigarami* trick aside, his actual skill was no ordinary thing.

## The match with Yoshida Katsunosuke

The episode that shows what his presence cost the academy is his challenge to the instructor
[Yoshida Katsunosuke](yoshida-katsunosuke.md). Yoshida — himself a Hōzōin-ryū man and a
pupil of Inō Ichiunsai — was challenged to a bout and, as the source puts it without ceremony, had of
course no prospect of winning. The matter was not resolved on the floor. The superintendent, Kugai
Inaba-no-kami, stepped in and talked the thing down, and it was only just got through without an
incident.[^tsg39]

That is the point of the anecdote. A *sewa-kokoroe* could beat a *shihan-yaku* so plainly that the
academy's senior officer had to intervene to protect the rank rather than let the result stand.

## The match with Takahashi, and the end of the technique

Kinpei was afterwards appointed *kyōju-kata sewa-kokoroe*, and by the chronicler's account wielded
considerable authority in the post. Then, on the twenty-seventh of the fourth month (the source gives no year),
[Takahashi Kenzaburō](deishu.md) — the later Deishū — took up duty, already spoken of as
the finest spear in the realm. Kinpei challenged him at once, on the reasoning that to beat the man
called first in the realm was to become first in the realm himself, and went in intending the usual
technique.

Takahashi had heard about the *ashigarami* in advance. After a crossing or two of the spears Kinpei
prostrated himself and said it had been a most obliging bout; and it is related that from that day
the foot-entanglement was given up.[^tsg40]

The school's own telling of this bout, in which Deishū beats Kinpei using Kinpei's own technique
against him, is the more satisfying story. The period account is plainer: Takahashi simply knew what
was coming.
