## Summary

Kubota Sugane ([窪田清音](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%AA%AA%E7%94%B0%E6%B8%85%E9%9F%B3), 1791–1867) was a hatamoto military scientist, swordsman and prolific writer
on the martial arts, and one of the six men appointed *tōdori* (頭取; director) when the Kōbusho
opened its doors in Ansei 2 (1855). He was the senior figure of the martial world of his generation,
credited with thousands of students in military science and hundreds in the martial arts, and with
something like a hundred and thirty works. Two things complicate his place in a register of
swordsmen: the post he actually held at the academy was in *heigaku* (兵学; military science) rather
than in any weapons department, and the sources disagree about which spear line he carried.

## The Kōbusho appointment

He appears in the founding cohort of Ansei 2 (1855)/2/5 — *tōdori* Udono, Odani, Morikawa, Ōkusa,
Hasegawa and Kubota — in both of the compilations that record it, the *Tōkyō shishi gaihen* volume on
the Kōbusho and the *Rikugun rekishi*.[^founding] The former prints him as 「寄合窪田助太郎清音」 (*yoriai* Kubota Suketarō Sugane),
which is what ties the roster's *Kubota Suketarō* to the author Kubota Sugane; a later page of the
same volume gives Suketarō (助太郎) and Gendayū (源太夫) together.[^names] He was among those who received the shogunal
gifts distributed to the senior staff.

His teaching post there was in military science, on the strength of the Yamaga-ryū strategy he
headed, so it is not clear that he taught any of his weapon arts at the academy. That his school had
a presence on the staff is nonetheless documented: the *Fujiokaya nikki*, quoted in the same source,
lists among the *kyōju-kata* one Ōta Rokurōzaemon (太田六郎左衛門) of the *koshōgumi*, entered as a
[Tamiya-ryū](../ryuha/tamiya-ryu.md) pupil of Kubota Suketarō.[^ota]

## Tamiya-ryū

Shimokawa's 1925 survey, describing the *Kenpō sōsho* (剣法叢書), names him **tenth-generation head of
Tamiya-ryū** (田宮流第十世) and dates the bulk of that collection to what he transmitted to Horie
Shirō (堀江四郎) between Tenpō 12 and 15 (1841–1844) — twenty-five essays on the art, in four
manuscript fascicles.[^sosho] The branch he carried is accordingly known as *Kubota-ha Tamiya-ryū*
(窪田派田宮流); he demonstrated its *iai* before Shōgun Tokugawa Ieyoshi in Tenpō 12 (1841). His
instruction was noted for an unusual emphasis on *shissō* (疾走; sprinting), so that the swordsman
could deliver full power under any condition.

The *Kenpō sōsho* is a separate work from the *Kenpō ryakki* (剣法略記) of Tenpō 10 (1839), the
seven-section technical treatise printed in the *Bujutsu sōsho* and treated at length in
the chapter on the *Kenpō ryakki*, in which he refers to himself by the name Sugane in the first person.

## Two open questions

The *Tōkyō shishi gaihen* art-set gives his spear as **Muhen-mukyoku-ryū** (無辺無極流), not the
[Hōzōin-ryū](../ryuha/hozoin-ryu.md) attributed to him in some earlier accounts; the conflict is recorded here and not
resolved. And the *tsūshō* 助太郎 is more likely *Suketarō* than *Sukedayū*, but the reading is not
settled by any source consulted.

He should not be confused with the jūjutsu teacher Kubota Shigekatsu (窪田鎮勝) of Kyūshin-ryū
(扱心流), adopted into the shogunal-retainer Kubota house from the Eguchi of Higo, with whom he is
occasionally conflated.
