Mitsuhashi Kan'ichirō (三橋鑑一郎, 1841–1909) was a Keishichō and Butokukai swordsman of the [Musashi-ryū](../ryuha/musashi-ryu.md) (武蔵流) — a two-sword exponent nicknamed "Kani no Mitsuhashi" (蟹の三橋, "Mitsuhashi the crab") — who received one of the Butokukai's first hanshi titles and helped draft the forms that preceded the national kendō kata.[^source]

Born the son of Satō Jidayū, a Tōgun-ryū (東軍流) instructor of the Mikawa Okazaki domain, he first learned his father's art; at eighteen he was adopted into the Mitsuhashi house and took up Musashi-ryū under Shingū Yajibei, and at twenty-one he entered the [Kyōshin Meichi-ryū](../ryuha/kyoshin-meichi-ryu.md) Shigakukan under Momonoi Shunzō. In 1865 he received full license in both Tōgun-ryū and Musashi-ryū and became the ninth head of the latter, celebrated as the Okazaki domain's prodigy and repeatedly commended by its lord ([jawiki_mitsuhashi](../references.md#jawiki_mitsuhashi); [jawiki_musashiryu](../references.md#jawiki_musashiryu)).

In the Meiji police he stood at the top grade of the 1888 sewakari register. At the first Butokusai in 1895 he beat [Takayama Minesaburō](takayama-minesaburo.md) and took a seirenshō, and in 1903 he was named one of the Butokukai's first hanshi. In 1906 he joined Watanabe Noboru and the other first-generation hanshi to frame the Dai Nippon Butokukai kenjutsu-kata — the direct forerunner of the Dai Nippon Teikoku Kendō Kata of 1911–12 ([jawiki_mitsuhashi](../references.md#jawiki_mitsuhashi); [jawiki_butokukai](../references.md#jawiki_butokukai)). In 1909, the year of his death, he published the *Kendō hiyō* (剣道秘要), which carried his annotations to Miyamoto Musashi's *Gorin no sho*; his adopted son Mitsuhashi Hidezō became a kendō hanshi and a professor at the Tokyo Higher Normal School.[^kata]

Mitsuhashi's Musashi-ryū (武蔵流) — the Okazaki line descending from Kusuda Enseki, a pupil of a Musashi disciple — is documented only lightly, and its relation to the wider Niten Ichi-ryū tradition is unresolved. His specific hand in the 1906 kenjutsu-kata, as against Watanabe Noboru and the other drafters, is not detailed in the available sources.
