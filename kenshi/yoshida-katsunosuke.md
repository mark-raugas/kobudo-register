## Summary

Yoshida Katsunosuke (吉田勝之助) was a [Hōzōin-ryū](../ryuha/hozoin-ryu.md) spear instructor at the Kōbusho who rose from
*kyōju-kata shutsuyaku* to *shihan-yaku* over the course of Bunkyū 2–3 (1862–63). He is one of the few
Kōbusho teachers whose own teacher is named in a source, and he is the instructor at the center of
the academy's most awkward recorded bout.

## School and teacher

The *Fujiokaya nikki*, quoted in the institutional history of the Kōbusho, enters him among the
*kyōju-kata* as a Hōzōin-ryū man of the *shinban* company of Matsudaira Shinano-no-kami and a pupil of
Inō Ichiunsai (伊能一雲斎).[^fujiokaya] Inō does not appear on the academy's staff, so this is one of
the two pupillages in that list reaching outside the institution — the other being Katsu Kenzaburō's
under Nagao Kohei, both of them serving instructors.

## Career

He was appointed *shihan-yaku-nami* on Bunkyū 2 (1862)/12/14, coming from the post of Kōbusho
*kyōju-kata shutsuyaku*, and *shihan-yaku* with the standing of *nimaru rusui-kaku* on Bunkyū 3
(1863)/10/3.[^rk152] He also appears in the spear lists of the Kōbusho roster and among the
*kyōju-kata* of the Ansei period.[^roster]

One discrepancy stands unresolved: his guard unit is given as the *shinban* company of **Matsudaira**
Shinano-no-kami in the *Fujiokaya nikki* list and in the roster chapter, but as that of **Murakami**
Shinano-no-kami in the *shihan-yaku* register. The Matsudaira reading has two witnesses to the
register's one.

## The bout with Ido Kinpei

[Ido Kinpei](ido-kinpei.md) — a *sewa-kokoroe* in the same department and a pupil of
[Komai Hangorō](komai-hangoro.md) — had been beating the teaching staff at will with the
foot-entanglement described in his own record. He challenged Yoshida to a match.

The institutional history is blunt about the prospects: Yoshida had, of course, no chance of winning.
What followed was not a bout but a rescue. The superintendent Kugai Inaba-no-kami intervened, talked
the affair down, and the thing was only barely got through without an incident.[^tsg39]

The episode is usually cited for what it says about Ido. It says as much about the institution: when a
supervisory assistant could beat a *shihan-yaku* in front of the hall, the academy's senior officer
protected the rank rather than let the floor decide. Yoshida is the man that protection was for, and
it is the only personal detail about him the sources preserve.
